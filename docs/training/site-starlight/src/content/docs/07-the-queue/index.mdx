<p>This is the capstone chapter. Chapter 6 got a single headless run to complete without anyone
present; this chapter adds the queue in front of it — the dark-factory chain that takes a piece of
work from an interactive interview, through a spec and tickets, past the one human gate
(`/triage` writing `Status: ready-for-agent`), and into `just watch`, the scan-claim-dispatch-resolve
loop that picks the next unblocked ticket and runs the full SDLC chain on it with nobody watching.
The five lessons walk that chain in order and end with you running it yourself, once, on a ticket you
filed.</p>

## What this chapter assumes

<p>It builds directly on Chapter 6's going-dark vocabulary and does not re-explain it:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Bootstrap vocabulary</strong> (<a href="/06-going-dark/lessons/0001-bootstrap-vocabulary/">Chapter 6</a>) —
  the interview runs once, interactively, and only its <em>result</em> (`CONTEXT.md`, `docs/adr/`) is
  read headless. The triage gate in this chapter is the same shape applied to feasibility and
  compliance: judge once, by a human; consume only the written result.</li>
  <li><strong>The two headless problems</strong> (<a href="/06-going-dark/lessons/0002-the-two-headless-problems/">Chapter 6</a>) —
  an interview with no one to answer it, and a plan re-implementing what already exists. "The judgment
  call stays interactive" is Part D's refusal to recreate the first of those inside the loop.</li>
  <li>A wired factory from Chapters 1–4: gates proven to fail, a roster in `sssf.config.yaml`,
  `adw_simple_sdlc.py` runnable by hand via `just simple-sdlc`.</li>
</ul>

<Callout kind="trap" label="Check which repo you're standing in">
<p>The `[run]` steps in this chapter drive `just watch`, which needs a <em>stamped</em> factory: a
working tree with a `justfile`, `adws/adw_watch.py`, `adws/adw_sssf_config/sssf.config.yaml`, a
`docs/agents/issue-tracker.md` whose first line says `Local Markdown`, and a `.scratch/` directory —
i.e. the Python playground repo from <a href="/01-sssf-fundamentals/reference/0004-wiring-your-factory/">Wiring Your Factory</a>,
not the skill-source fork. The source the lessons quote lives in the fork,
`joystick/super-simple-software-factory`, at `.claude/skills/sssf/templates/adws/adw_watch.py`; the
copy `just watch` actually runs is your stamped `adws/adw_watch.py`. If the two differ, the stamped
one wins for anything you observe, and every `[run]` step below assumes a <strong>clean working
tree</strong> — `run_once()` refuses to claim on a dirty one.</p>
</Callout>

## Lessons

<ol>
  <li><a href="/07-the-queue/lessons/0000-the-chain-named-once/">The Chain Named Once</a> — grill-with-docs,
  to-spec, to-tickets, triage, and watch: the whole dark-factory chain, named once in order, so each
  later lesson can point back to a single reference instead of re-deriving it.</li>
  <li><a href="/07-the-queue/lessons/0001-triage-as-sole-promoter/">Triage as Sole Promoter</a> — only
  `/triage` may promote a ticket to `ready-for-agent`; why that judgment stays interactive on purpose,
  and the sharp edge where a hand-run `/to-spec` or `/to-tickets` self-promotes past it.</li>
  <li><a href="/07-the-queue/lessons/0002-filing-checkpoints/">Filing Checkpoints</a> — a skill
  claiming to have written a ticket is not the same as the queue seeing it; the six mandatory
  checkpoints that catch a filing that silently didn't take.</li>
  <li><a href="/07-the-queue/lessons/0003-just-watch-concretely/">just watch, Concretely</a> — the
  scan-claim-dispatch-resolve cycle read from `adw_watch.py` itself: the frontier rule,
  `Status:`/`Blocked by:` parsing, and what happens on success versus failure.</li>
  <li><a href="/07-the-queue/lessons/0004-capstone-run-a-queue-end-to-end/">Capstone — Run a Queue End to End</a> —
  grill, spec, ticket, triage, file, checkpoint, then `just watch --once`, and read the resolved
  ticket's trail back to a commit.</li>
</ol>
